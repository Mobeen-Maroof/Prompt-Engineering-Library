# Day 10 – Decomposition: Self-Ask, Least-to-Most & Tree of Thoughts

## Objective

The purpose of this exercise is to understand how complex questions can be solved by breaking them into smaller and easier sub-problems before producing the final answer.

---

# Problem 1

## Plain Prompt

Who was older at death: the first person to walk on the Moon or the author of *A Brief History of Time*?

### Observation

The direct prompt provides an answer immediately without explicitly showing how the information was gathered.

## Self-Ask Prompt

The problem was divided into the following sub-questions:

1. Who was the first person to walk on the Moon?
2. How old was that person at death?
3. Who wrote *A Brief History of Time*?
4. How old was that author at death?
5. Compare both ages.

### Result

The Self-Ask approach produced a more transparent and reliable answer because each fact was verified before comparison.

# Which decomposition style worked best?

Self-Ask worked best for these questions because each required gathering multiple facts before making a comparison. Separating the question into smaller sub-questions reduced the chance of missing information and made the reasoning easier to follow.

---

# Conclusion

This activity demonstrated that decomposition techniques improve AI reasoning by dividing difficult questions into manageable parts. Self-Ask is especially useful for multi-hop questions that require collecting several pieces of information before reaching the final answer.
